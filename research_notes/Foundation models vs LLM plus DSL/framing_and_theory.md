# Framing and Theory: Domain Foundation Models ("bitter lesson") vs General LLM + Formal Language + Checker

Context: talk 2x2 = x-axis abundant data (real or simulated); y-axis formal language + automated checker exists. Code = both; math (Lean) and CAD (OpenSCAD) = language+checker; proteins/robotics = data; small-molecule drug discovery = neither.

Bottom line: I found **no single source that draws this exact 2x2**. The pieces are well covered, though. (1) Sutton's Bitter Lesson says general methods that scale with compute win. Its two scalable methods are *search and learning*, and search needs a verifier. (2) Sutton's own 2001 essay, Jason Wei's "verifier's law" (2025), Karpathy's "Software 2.0 automates what you can verify" (2025) and Tao's comments on Lean all name verifiability as what decides where AI moves fastest. (3) Results from 2023 to 2026 keep showing generalist LLMs beating domain-pretrained models on text-based domain tasks (Medprompt vs Med-PaLM 2, GPT-4 vs BloombergGPT). (4) For the "neither" quadrant, the main answer in the literature and in industry is to *manufacture* data and verification with autonomous or lab-in-the-loop experiments (Periodic Labs, Lila, Amodei's "marginal returns to intelligence").

## Q1: The Bitter Lesson and its critiques. Does it say anything about data-scarce domains?

### Takeaway
The Bitter Lesson (Sutton, March 2019) is about *compute-scalable general methods (search + learning)*, not specifically about "training on lots of domain data". It does not address data-scarce domains. The critiques (Brooks 2019, Marcus 2025, the chemistry/science community) argue that human-designed structure, data cost and symbolic components still matter. In 2025 Sutton argued that LLMs themselves are *not* bitter-lesson-pilled, because they learn from human data rather than experience.

### Cited Findings
- **Sutton, "The Bitter Lesson" (Mar 13, 2019).** Thesis: "general methods that leverage computation are ultimately the most effective, and by a large margin". The two methods that "seem to scale arbitrarily" are **search and learning**. It closes by saying we want agents that "can discover like we can, not which contain what we have discovered". Examples: chess, Go, speech, vision. It does not discuss data scarcity or settings where compute cannot substitute for data. — [incompleteideas.net](http://www.incompleteideas.net/IncIdeas/BitterLesson.html) (the fetch failed with an SSL error, so quotes are from the well-known text; check the wording before putting it on a slide)
- **Sutton, "Self-Verification, The Key to AI" (Nov 15, 2001).** "An AI system can create and maintain knowledge only to the extent that it can verify that knowledge itself." Example: Deep Blue's search *verifies* that a move is good. This is the same author as the Bitter Lesson putting verification at the center 18 years earlier, which bridges both axes of the 2x2. — [incompleteideas.net/IncIdeas/KeytoAI.html](http://incompleteideas.net/IncIdeas/KeytoAI.html)
- **Rodney Brooks, "A Better Lesson" (Mar 19, 2019).** CNNs build in human-designed translational invariance: "the very essence of CNNs is that the front end of the network is designed by humans". "Massive data sets are not at all what humans need to learn things so something is missing." His better lesson: "we have to take into account the total cost of any solution, and that so far they have all required substantial amounts of human ingenuity." — [rodneybrooks.com/a-better-lesson](https://rodneybrooks.com/a-better-lesson/)
- **Gary Marcus, "How o3 and Grok 4 Accidentally Vindicated Neurosymbolic AI" (Jul 13, 2025).** Argues that LLMs boosted by Python and other tools are neurosymbolic, and that DeepMind's AlphaGo, AlphaFold, AlphaProof and AlphaGeometry are neurosymbolic hybrids. — [garymarcus.substack.com](https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/how-o3-and-grok-4-accidentally-vindicated). Earlier essay: "AlphaProof, AlphaGeometry, ChatGPT, and why the future of AI is neurosymbolic" (2024) — [link](https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/alphaproof-alphageometry-chatgpt). Academic version: Belle & Marcus, "The Future Is Neuro-Symbolic" (AAAI) — [PDF](https://www.vaishakbelle.org/attachments/Belle_Marcus_AAAI-2.pdf)
- **Sutton on the Dwarkesh Podcast (Sep 26, 2025), "LLMs are a dead end".** LLMs learn "from training data… not from experience". Dwarkesh's steelman is that continual on-the-job learning needs a new architecture. — [dwarkesh.com/p/richard-sutton](https://www.dwarkesh.com/p/richard-sutton); [Dwarkesh on X](https://x.com/dwarkesh_sp/status/1971606180553183379)
- **Silver & Sutton, "Welcome to the Era of Experience" (2025, DeepMind; chapter for *Designing an Intelligence*, MIT Press).** The next generation of agents will gain superhuman abilities by learning mainly from their own experience, with rewards *grounded in the environment* rather than human prejudgment. That makes environments and reward signals, not human data, the scarce resource. — [PDF](https://storage.googleapis.com/deepmind-media/Era-of-Experience%20/The%20Era%20of%20Experience%20Paper.pdf)
- **Science/chemistry view on data scarcity.** The Spiers Memorial Lecture on AI for chemistry and materials notes the bitter lesson "does not mean that imposing inductive bias… is never good". Unlike language and images, "chemical data is scarce and costs real-world experiments to obtain", so algorithms must use limited data efficiently. — [arXiv 2409.10304](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.10304)
- **Other responses:** "The Bitter Lesson is Misunderstood" (Chakrabarti) — [obviouslywrong.org](https://www.obviouslywrong.org/p/the-bitter-lesson-is-misunderstood); "The Occasional Bitter Lesson" (digital humanities: "sometimes sweet, sometimes bitter, and sometimes simply inapplicable") — [deep-culture.org](https://deep-culture.org/the-occasional-bitter-lesson-ai-engineering-and-expert-knowledge-in-the-digital-humanities/); empirical CVPR study "Learning the Bitter Lesson" — [arXiv 2410.09649](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.09649)

### Inferences
- Read literally, the Bitter Lesson supports *both* sides of the talk's dichotomy. "Learning" is the domain-data route. "Search" means generate-and-check, which needs a cheap verifier, i.e. the DSL+checker route. Its point is that the method should be *general*, not that it should be data-hungry. A strong talk line: Sutton 2001 (verification) + Sutton 2019 (search + learning) together predict the 2x2.
- Sutton's own 2025 position (LLMs aren't bitter-lesson-pilled; the future is experience) puts the "neither" quadrant's answer at *building environments that produce experience*, i.e. labs and simulators.

### Gaps
- I did not get a verbatim fetch of the Bitter Lesson because of the site's SSL error. Wording is from well-known text.
- I found no Sutton writing that addresses data-scarce domains like drug discovery directly.

## Q2: Verifiability determines where AI progresses fastest (Wei, Karpathy, RLVR, Tao)

### Takeaway
Since 2025 there has been a clear, citable thesis: **AI will rapidly master whatever is cheap to verify**. Wei calls it "Verifier's Law" and Karpathy calls it the "Software 2.0 automates what you can verify" predicate. RLVR is the machinery behind it, and Tao applies it to math through Lean. This maps directly onto the y-axis of the 2x2.

### Cited Findings
- **Jason Wei, "Asymmetry of verification and verifier's law" (Jul 15, 2025).** "The ease of training AI to solve a task is proportional to how verifiable the task is. All tasks that are possible to solve and easy to verify will be solved by AI." He lists five properties of a good verifier: objective truth, fast to verify, scalable verification, low noise, and continuous reward. Examples: Sudoku, building websites, BrowseComp, AlphaEvolve. — [jasonwei.net](https://www.jasonwei.net/blog/asymmetry-of-verification-and-verifiers-law)
- **Karpathy, "Software 2.0" (Nov 2017)** is the original essay on neural nets as programs found by optimization. — [karpathy.medium.com](https://karpathy.medium.com/software-2-0-a64152b37c35)
- **Karpathy, X post (Nov 16, 2025).** AI is best understood as a new computing paradigm (Software 2.0). "Software 1.0 easily automates what you can specify. Software 2.0 easily automates what you can verify." "If a task/job is verifiable, then it is optimizable directly or via reinforcement learning, and a neural net can be trained to work extremely well." The environment must be **resettable, efficient and rewardable**. Verifiable tasks (math, code, puzzles) progress fast, possibly beyond top experts, while creative and strategic tasks and those needing real-world context and common sense lag. — [X post](https://x.com/karpathy/status/1990116666194456651); quoted by [Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Nov/16/andrej-karpathy/)
- **Terence Tao (2024–2026).** "In almost any other application, the biggest Achilles heel of AI is that it makes unverifiable mistakes. But in mathematics, almost uniquely, you can automatically check the output" (*Nature*, May 2026). AI output should be combined "with more traditional and reliable verification methods, in order to filter out hallucinations" (*Notices AMS*, Jan 2025). Formal verification via Lean "enables human-AI collaboration at scale" by filtering untrustworthy inputs. By 2025–26, autoformalization had "essentially emptied" the formalization queues on at least one project. — [Tao's living summary of his AI views](https://teorth.github.io/tao-web/ai-views.html); [interview, Jul 2026](https://teorth.github.io/tao-web/ai-views-interview.html); [Quanta, Jun 2026](https://www.quantamagazine.org/how-terry-tao-became-an-evangelist-for-ai-in-math-20260608/)
- **RLVR beyond verifiable domains:** "Crossing the Reward Bridge: Expanding RL with Verifiable Rewards Across Diverse Domains" (Mar 2025) — [arXiv 2503.23829](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.23829); "Rubrics as Rewards: RL Beyond Verifiable Domains" (Jul 2025; medicine and science) — [arXiv 2507.17746](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17746); Tanay Jaipuria, "RL Beyond the Verifiable" (essay; examples of hard-to-verify tasks include a Mars mission plan and CRISPR-scale discovery) — [tanayj.com](https://www.tanayj.com/p/rl-beyond-the-verifiable)

### Inferences
- Wei's five properties make a useful rubric for placing domains on the y-axis. Code tests, Lean, and OpenSCAD plus a geometry/physics check are fast, scalable, objective and low-noise. Small-molecule efficacy in vivo is slow, expensive, noisy and not scalable. That is why drug discovery sits in the "neither" quadrant even though partial checkers exist (docking scores, ADMET predictors, which are noisy proxies).
- The DSL matters because it *turns a domain into something verifiable*. Lean does this for math, and OpenSCAD plus CAD kernels do it for geometry. The DSL is where the checker attaches.

### Gaps
- I did not verify Karpathy's June 2025 "Software 3.0" YC talk from a primary source in this session (it is commonly cited as "English is the programming language").
- I found no Epoch AI piece specifically on verifiability; I did not search exhaustively.

## Q3: Generalist vs specialist. Do general LLMs overtake domain-pretrained models?

### Takeaway
On text-mediated domain tasks the record from 2023 to 2026 consistently favors generalist frontier LLMs, sometimes with clever prompting or tools, over domain-pretrained LLMs. Specialists keep an edge on structured prediction tasks and, by implication, on non-text modalities such as protein sequence and structure, where domain foundation models like AlphaFold and ESM remain standard.

### Cited Findings
- **Nori et al. (Microsoft), "Can Generalist Foundation Models Outcompete Special-Purpose Tuning? Case Study in Medicine" (Nov 28, 2023), Medprompt.** GPT-4 plus a composed prompting strategy beat specialist Med-PaLM 2 "by a significant margin with an order of magnitude fewer calls". It cut the error rate on MedQA by 27% and was the first to exceed 90%. The method generalizes to electrical engineering, ML, law, accounting, nursing and other exams. — [arXiv 2311.16452](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.16452)
- **Li et al., "Are ChatGPT and GPT-4 General-Purpose Solvers for Financial Text Analytics?" (May 2023; EMNLP Industry 2023).** GPT-4 beat the domain-pretrained BloombergGPT on almost all financial text tasks. The exception was NER (GPT-4 56.71 F1 vs BloombergGPT 60.82). Fine-tuned domain models stay ahead on structured prediction (NER, relation extraction). — [arXiv 2305.05862](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05862); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-industry.39/)
- **"General-purpose large language models outperform specialized clinical AI tools on medical benchmarks" (*Nature Medicine*, 2026, per search snippet).** Compared clinical AI tools against GPT-5.2, Gemini 3.1 Pro and Claude Opus 4.6. — [PubMed 42286322](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42286322/) (I could not fetch the abstract, so details are unverified)
- **"LLMs Outperform Experts on Challenging Biology Benchmarks" (May 2025).** 27 frontier LLMs on 8 biology benchmarks. VCT-Text scores rose about 4x and CloningScenarios about 2.6x between 2022 and 2025, and several models now match or beat expert baselines. — [arXiv 2505.06108](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.06108)
- **Counterpoint, general LLMs alone struggle in chemistry, so tools are needed.** ChemCrow (Bran, Schwaller et al.; preprint Apr 2023, *Nature Machine Intelligence* May 2024): "LLMs… struggle with chemistry-related problems". Adding expert-designed tools (13 in the paper, 18 in later versions) to GPT-4 enabled autonomous planning of syntheses (DEET, organocatalysts) and helped discover a chromophore. This is the clearest instance of "general LLM + domain tools" in chemistry. — [Nature MI](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-024-00832-8)

### Inferences
- The pattern is that the generalist wins **where the domain is expressed in text or a symbolic language the LLM can manipulate**. Specialists hold on where the "language" is a raw physical modality such as sequences, structures or sensor streams, with abundant data and no human-readable checker. That matches the talk's x-axis-only quadrant (proteins, robotics).
- ChemCrow shows that for small molecules the "language" (SMILES, reaction tools) exists, but the checker for what really matters (does the drug work?) does not. The tools give partial verification at best.

### Gaps
- I found no rigorous head-to-head between general LLM + tools and domain foundation models (e.g. ESM or AlphaFold-class) on protein design. That comparison would test the 2x2 directly.

## Q4: "LLM as general reasoner + domain tools/languages" as the strategy for science

### Takeaway
The strongest advocates are Tao (math via Lean), Marcus (neurosymbolic), Amodei (AI as a "virtual biologist" running experiments, limited by "marginal returns to intelligence"), and builders such as ChemCrow and FutureHouse. Amodei's framing is the most useful for the talk because it names the factors that are complementary to intelligence: data and the speed of the physical world.

### Cited Findings
- **Dario Amodei, "Machines of Loving Grace" (Oct 2024).** "We should be talking about the marginal returns to intelligence, and trying to figure out what the other factors are that are complementary to intelligence". The limiting factors he names include the speed of the physical world, need for data, intrinsic complexity, human constraints and physical laws. On experiments: "Cells and animals run at a fixed speed so experiments on them take a certain amount of time which may be irreducible." He describes AI that will "perform, direct, and improve upon nearly everything biologists do", not "merely a tool to analyze data". He says little about specialist vs generalist models and assumes powerful AI will be "similar to today's LLMs". — [darioamodei.com](https://www.darioamodei.com/essay/machines-of-loving-grace)
- **Tao: Lean as the trust layer.** Lean "formalizes a proof a human already has" and is "not all that useful in discovering new proofs" on its own. Its value is filtering AI output, which enables "industrial-scale mathematics… like a modern supply chain". He frames AI as best used for red-teaming (checking) rather than blue-teaming (building) when it is unreliable (Jul 2025). — [Tao AI views](https://teorth.github.io/tao-web/ai-views.html)
- **Marcus:** tool-augmented LLMs (Python etc.) are evidence that neurosymbolic AI is winning. — [Substack, Jul 2025](https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/how-o3-and-grok-4-accidentally-vindicated)
- **FutureHouse** has cited ChemCrow as the template for an "AI biologist". — [Chemistry World](https://www.chemistryworld.com/news/future-house-wants-to-build-an-ai-biologist-theyre-looking-to-a-chemistry-llm-for-inspiration/4018506.article)

### Inferences
- Amodei's "marginal returns to intelligence" is effectively the x-axis argument. A smarter general model doesn't help much when the bottleneck is data or experiment speed, and that is the hardest quadrant.

- I did not check DeepMind's or OpenAI's official "AI for science" essays (e.g. DeepMind's 2024 "A new golden age of discovery" essay, OpenAI for Science 2025) in this session.

## Q5: The "no data AND no verifier" quadrant (e.g. small-molecule drug discovery)

### Takeaway
No source names the quadrant explicitly in 2x2 terms. In practice the consensus answer is to **build a loop that creates both data and verification**: autonomous or lab-in-the-loop experimentation (Periodic Labs, Lila Sciences), simulators, and closed DMTA (design-make-test-analyze) cycles. This is Silver & Sutton's "era of experience" applied to wet labs. The lab remains the bottleneck.

### Cited Findings
- **Periodic Labs (founded 2025 by Liam Fedus, a ChatGPT co-creator, and Ekin Dogus Cubuk of DeepMind materials).** Their bet is that models trained on internet text have exhausted the available data, and that progress in physical science needs *new experimental data* from autonomous labs. The literature itself is inadequate: noisy labels (e.g. formation enthalpy), unpublished negative results, and uncertainty that only an experiment can resolve. — [MIT Technology Review, Dec 15, 2025](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/12/15/1129210/ai-materials-science-discovery-startups-investment/); [a16z announcement](https://a16z.com/announcement/investing-in-periodic-labs/)
- **Lila Sciences ("scientific superintelligence" + autonomous labs)** claims more than 10 trillion tokens of scientific-reasoning data generated against experimental results. This is a company claim and unverified. — [lila.ai](https://www.lila.ai/); [Contrary Research](https://research.contrary.com/company/lila-sciences)
- **Drug discovery specifics.** DMTA cycles lack large curated public data because of confidentiality and inconsistent reporting. Training sets are mostly positive-only. "Every AI-generated candidate still needs to be validated in the lab". The competitive advantage moves to closed loops that keep high-quality data flowing in. — [MIT Tech Review, "Closing the data loop in AI-driven drug discovery" (Jul 27, 2026)](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/07/27/1139667/closing-the-data-loop-in-ai-driven-drug-discovery/); [Bio-IT World, "As AI Reshapes Drug Discovery the Lab Remains the Bottleneck" (Jun 9, 2026)](https://www.bio-itworld.com/news/2026/06/09/as-ai-reshapes-drug-discovery-the-lab-remains-the-bottleneck); [Applied Clinical Trials](https://www.appliedclinicaltrialsonline.com/view/the-evolving-role-of-ai-in-shifting-the-bottleneck-in-early-drug-discovery); review "Small data, big challenges… data-limited drug discovery" — [Adv Drug Deliv Rev 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0169409X25002479)
- **Soft verifiers as a partial fix:** rubric-based and model-based rewards extend RL into domains that aren't verifiable (medicine, science). — [Rubrics as Rewards, arXiv 2507.17746](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17746)
- **Skeptical take:** a Japanese essay, "Billions flowing into AI x Labs: $7.5B valuation, zero discoveries", questions whether autonomous-lab startups have produced discoveries yet. — [note.com](https://note.com/all_zero/n/n3560a02564fd?hl=en) (opinion piece, low authority)

### Inferences
- There are three ways out of the "neither" quadrant, each moving along one axis. (a) Move right by generating data: high-throughput or autonomous labs, simulation (physics-based FEP, MD). (b) Move up by building checkers: better proxy verifiers such as docking, ADMET models and rubric rewards, accepting that they are noisy and fail Wei's "low noise" property. (c) Do both in a closed loop, which is the "era of experience" / lab-in-the-loop answer. Dividing it this way is my synthesis, not something taken from one source.
- Simulated data counts on the x-axis only if the simulator is faithful. For small molecules, binding affinity simulation is costly and imperfect, which is one reason the quadrant stays hard.

### Gaps
- I found no paper or essay that explicitly draws the data × verifier 2x2 or names this quadrant. The framing appears to be novel to the talk, or at least not prominent.
- I did not find Dwarkesh or Epoch AI transcripts specifically about drug discovery verifiability.
