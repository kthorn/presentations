# Generalist LLMs + Tools/Languages vs Domain Foundation Models in Small-Molecule Chemistry and Drug Discovery

*Research date: 2026-10-06. All claims below carry an inline source URL. Where I could not verify a number, it is listed under Gaps rather than stated.*

## Q1. Generalist LLM agents in chemistry (ChemCrow, Coscientist, FutureHouse, AI co-scientist, TxGemma) — and head-to-head evidence vs specialists

### Takeaway
The strongest 2025–2026 evidence is that a *general* reasoning LLM plus tools/verifiers is a highly competitive substrate: FutureHouse's ether0 took a general 24B instruct model with **no chemistry pretraining** and beat both frontier generalists and chemistry-specific LLMs on open-answer molecular design, with ~10x better data efficiency than a task-specific transformer. But agentic systems' published evaluations remain small, and tool-augmented agents can be *worse* than the base LLM on reasoning-heavy chemistry questions.

### Cited Findings
- **ether0** (FutureHouse, announced June 2025; arXiv 2506.17238, "Training a Scientific Reasoning Model for Chemistry"): a 24B open-weight model fine-tuned from Mistral-Small-24B-Instruct via RL (GRPO) on chemistry tasks with *verifiable* rewards; "one of the first LLMs trained for scientific reasoning via RL without prior chemistry-specific pretraining." — [FutureHouse announcement](https://www.futurehouse.org/research-announcements/ether0-a-scientific-reasoning-model-for-chemistry); [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.17238)
- ether0 training scale: ~640,730 chemistry problems across 375 tasks (solubility optimization, retrosynthesis, receptor binding, scent prediction, etc.); pipeline = SFT on chain-of-thought → per-task RL "specialists" → distillation into one generalist → final all-task RL. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.17238)
- ether0 outperformed general-purpose LLMs (reported vs Claude 3.7, GPT-4.5), chemistry-specific models (e.g. ChemDFM), and human experts on open-answer molecular design tasks by **12–28% absolute accuracy**. — [FutureHouse announcement](https://www.futurehouse.org/research-announcements/ether0-a-scientific-reasoning-model-for-chemistry); secondary coverage [BiopharmaTrend](https://www.biopharmatrend.com/news/futurehouse-debuts-scientific-reasoning-model-trained-for-chemistry-1290/)
- **Data efficiency (the single most relevant datum for the DSL-vs-foundation-model question):** ether0 reached ~70% accuracy on reaction prediction from ~46,000 training examples, vs the Molecular Transformer's 64% trained on ~480,000 reactions — i.e. better accuracy from ~10x less data, because the reasoning prior transfers. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.17238)
- ether0 is Apache-2.0 on Hugging Face and is wired into FutureHouse's chemistry agent "Phoenix." — [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/futurehouse/ether0)
- **ChemCrow** (Bran et al., *Nature Machine Intelligence* 2024): GPT-4 + 18 expert-designed chemistry tools; the canonical "generalist LLM + tool layer" result. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.05376); [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11116106/)
- **Critique of the agentic evaluations:** ChemCrow was assessed on only **14 tasks** (mostly synthesis) and Coscientist on **6 tasks**; "these narrow assessments leave a large gap in our understanding of how tool-augmented agents perform across diverse chemistry tasks." — [ChemToolAgent / "Tooling or Not Tooling?", NAACL Findings 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.424.pdf); [arXiv 2411.07228](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.07228)
- **Tools are not free:** on general chemistry questions requiring fundamental knowledge and extended reasoning, tool-augmented agents **underperform the base LLM**, due to "delicate mistakes at intermediate stages... wrong logic and information oversight." Specific tool failures cited include inflexible input handling and output truncation in ChemCrow's web search. — [ChemToolAgent](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.424.pdf); [PMC version](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12765490/)
- **Robin** (FutureHouse multi-agent system, arXiv 2505.13400, May 2025): proposed 30 candidates for dry age-related macular degeneration, narrowed to 2, and nominated **ripasudil** (a glaucoma ROCK inhibitor) for dAMD via a novel mechanism — enhanced RPE phagocytosis — validated experimentally. — [arXiv 2505.13400](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.13400); [DeepLearning.ai The Batch](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/put-the-lab-in-the-loop)
- **Google AI co-scientist**: published in *Nature* ("Accelerating scientific discovery with Co-Scientist"); identified drug-repurposing candidates and synergistic combinations for AML validated in vitro, and in a liver-organoid experiment a co-scientist-suggested agent (vorinostat) cut the TGFβ-induced response by ~91%. — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10644-y); context/coverage [C&EN, May 2026](https://cen.acs.org/pharmaceuticals/drug-discovery/ai-companies-introduce-agent-based-research-tools/104/web/2026/05)
- Co-Scientist and Robin appeared **side by side in the same Nature issue** in the third week of May 2026; both teams emphasize humans remained in the loop for every wet-lab experiment. — [C&EN](https://cen.acs.org/pharmaceuticals/drug-discovery/ai-companies-introduce-agent-based-research-tools/104/web/2026/05); [Singularity Hub](https://singularityhub.com/2026/05/21/ai-lab-partners-are-rewiring-the-hunt-for-new-drugs/)
- **TxGemma** (Google, April 2025): 2B/9B/27B Gemma-2-based therapeutics models, successor to Tx-LLM (which was PaLM-2 finetuned on 709 datasets / 66 TDC tasks). TxGemma was retrained on the TDC suite with ~7M curated examples. Reported: improves over Tx-LLM on **45/66** tasks; **surpasses or matches best-in-class on 50/66** tasks; **exceeds specialist models on 26** tasks. — [TxGemma technical report](https://storage.googleapis.com/research-media/txgemma/txgemma-report.pdf); [Google Developers Blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/introducing-txgemma-open-models-improving-therapeutics-development/); [DeepMind publication page](https://deepmind.google/research/publications/153799/)
- TxGemma's own framing is explicitly hybrid: "Efficient and **Agentic** LLMs for Therapeutics" — i.e. a domain-tuned model intended to be *called by* an agent, not a replacement for one. — [TxGemma report](https://storage.googleapis.com/research-media/txgemma/txgemma-report.pdf)
- **Where generalist LLMs clearly lose:** cross-scale evaluations find LLM-based informatics "generally underperform traditional methods in terms of predictive accuracy and computational efficiency," with a persistent gap vs specialized GNNs, attributed to text-based reasoning failing to capture graph/topological structure. No evaluated model achieved meaningful performance on hard regressions; DMPK solubility was unsolved (negative R² for all models). — [The limits of bio-molecular modeling with LLMs: a cross-scale evaluation](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03361v1); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13457088/)
- Framing of the trade-off in the literature: "task-specific specialist models excel in familiar scenarios but completely lack cross-task generalization; generalist models show consistent cross-task performance but suffer from insufficient domain expertise," and "specialized computational tools are more reliable for calibrated numerical prediction and ADMET-like property estimation." — [cross-scale evaluation](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03361v1)

### Inferences
- The ether0 result is the closest thing to a direct answer to the user's open question, and it cuts a third way: *don't* pretrain a domain foundation model from scratch, and *don't* rely on prompting alone — take a general reasoning model and RL it against **verifiable** chemistry rewards. That requires exactly what a medchem-operations DSL would provide: a space of well-formed actions whose outcomes a checker can score.
- The division of labor that the evidence supports: generalist LLM for planning/reasoning/hypothesis selection; narrow specialist models (ADMET regressors, docking, affinity predictors) as *called tools*, because that is where LLMs measurably fail (calibrated numeric prediction).
- The ChemToolAgent finding (tools can make agents worse via intermediate-step errors) is an argument *for* a constrained DSL: it bounds the agent's action space instead of leaving free-form tool invocation.

### Gaps
- I did not find a clean, published head-to-head of "frontier general LLM + DSL/tools" vs "domain foundation model" on the *same* medchem optimization task with the same oracles — the comparisons that exist are either benchmark-level (TxGemma vs specialists on TDC) or generation-level (ether0 vs ChemDFM), not architecture-of-system-level.
- Microsoft and Anthropic/OpenAI life-science small-molecule work: I did not locate primary, verifiable 2025–2026 small-molecule-chemistry results from these within budget. Treat as unresearched rather than absent.

## Q2. Chemistry LLM benchmarks (ChemBench, ChemIQ) — frontier generalists vs chemists vs chemistry-specific models; trend over time

### Takeaway
On knowledge-and-reasoning benchmarks, frontier generalist LLMs now beat expert human chemists outright (ChemBench, *Nature Chemistry* 2025: the best model scored ~2x the top human). But on benchmarks built to require actual *molecular* manipulation (ChemIQ), even the best reasoning models top out near ~50–57%, and the reasoning/non-reasoning gap is enormous (50–57% vs 3–7%) — which is itself the strongest evidence that reasoning RL, not domain pretraining, is the active ingredient.

### Cited Findings
- **ChemBench** (Mirza et al., *Nature Chemistry*, 2025): >2,700 question–answer pairs; "leading LLMs outperform expert chemists in chemical knowledge and reasoning across diverse topics." — [Nature Chemistry](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41557-025-01815-x); [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12226332/); original preprint [arXiv 2404.01475](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2404.01475)
- Margin: OpenAI **o1** (leading model) scored roughly **double the top human** chemist's score; even the worst-performing model scored ~50% higher than the *average* human chemist. — [Chemistry World coverage](https://www.chemistryworld.com/news/large-language-models-are-better-than-humans-at-answering-chemistry-questions/4020562.article); [Nature Chemistry](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41557-025-01815-x)
- ChemBench's documented failure modes: models struggle on some *basic* tasks, give **overconfident** predictions, and show stark topic disparities — strong on general/technical topics, weak where specialist knowledge or **spatial chemical reasoning** is needed (safety, analytical chemistry). — [Nature Chemistry](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41557-025-01815-x); research briefing "Are large language models superhuman chemists?" — [Nature Chemistry briefing](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41557-025-01865-1)
- **ChemIQ** (Runcie et al., "Assessing the Chemical Intelligence of Large Language Models"; arXiv 2505.07735, published *J. Chem. Inf. Model.* 2025/2026): 816 questions on core organic chemistry, explicitly designed to test molecular comprehension and reasoning rather than recall. Three competencies: interpreting molecular structures; translating structures to concepts; reasoning with chemical theory. — [arXiv 2505.07735](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.07735); [JCIM](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/full/10.1021/acs.jcim.5c02145); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12801315/)
- ChemIQ is described as the first benchmark to test tasks like shortest atom-to-atom path, atom mapping between two representations of the same molecule, 2D NMR elucidation, and **Free-Wilson analysis** (i.e. literal medchem-SAR operations). — [arXiv 2505.07735](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.07735)
- ChemIQ results: reasoning models (o3-mini, Gemini 2.5 Pro, DeepSeek R1) answered **50–57%** correctly at their highest reasoning setting; **non-reasoning models scored 3–7%**. — [arXiv 2505.07735](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.07735); [JCIM](https://pubs.acs.org/doi/full/10.1021/acs.jcim.5c02145)
- TxGemma reported beating o3-mini on ChemBench variants by 5.6% (ChemBench-Preference) and 1.1% (ChemBench-Mini) — i.e. the domain model's edge over a frontier generalist on chemistry knowledge is small, single-digit. — [TxGemma report](https://storage.googleapis.com/research-media/txgemma/txgemma-report.pdf)
- Benchmark proliferation continues into 2026 (ChemVTS-Bench for visual-textual-symbolic reasoning; SupraBench for supramolecular chemistry; ChemReason-Bench for procedural experimental reasoning; MolecularIQ, which uses **symbolic verification on molecular graphs**), indicating the field is converging on verifiable, structure-grounded evaluation. — [ChemVTS-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.17909); [SupraBench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13477); [ChemReason-Bench, ACL 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1535/); [MolecularIQ, ICLR 2026](https://en.papernotes.org/ICLR2026/llm_reasoning/moleculariq_characterizing_chemical_reasoning_capabilities_through_symbolic_veri/)

### Inferences
- The ChemBench vs ChemIQ contrast is a clean talking point: generalist LLMs have *saturated chemical knowledge* relative to humans while remaining mediocre at *operating on molecular structure*. A DSL that externalizes structure manipulation (graph edits, atom mapping, transformation application) targets exactly the residual deficit, rather than trying to re-learn the knowledge an LLM already has.
- The 3–7% → 50–57% jump from non-reasoning to reasoning models on ChemIQ, with no change in chemistry-specific pretraining, is direct evidence that general reasoning capability — not domain corpora — drove the recent gains.
- Benchmarks are moving toward symbolic verification on molecular graphs (MolecularIQ), which is structurally the same thing as "a checker for a medchem DSL."

### Gaps
- I did not find a single longitudinal plot/table of ChemBench or ChemIQ scores by model release date; the "trend over time" is reconstructed from the o1/o3-mini/Gemini-2.5 data points above rather than from a published time series.

## Q3. Chemistry foundation models — does pretraining scale help under data scarcity? Critiques.

### Takeaway
The weight of 2025–2026 evidence is negative-to-mixed for *molecular* foundation models: multiple systematic studies find pretrained molecular embeddings give negligible or no gain over ECFP fingerprints with gradient-boosted trees/RF, and pretraining benefits plateau at a few hundred thousand molecules. The interesting 2026 twist is that **tabular** foundation models (TabPFN) on top of pretrained representations *do* win — which is a statement about few-shot learning under scarcity, not about chemical pretraining scale.

### Cited Findings
- A 2025 comparison of **25 pretrained molecular embedding models** found "nearly all neural models show negligible or no improvement over baseline ECFP molecular fingerprints" with traditional ensemble methods. — reported in [search synthesis]; primary candidate: [Deep Learning Foundation Models from Classical Molecular Descriptors (arXiv 2506.15792)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.15792) *(see Gaps — I could not pin the 25-model study to a single verified primary URL)*
- Pretraining-scale plateau: a 2025 study of transformers for ADMET found performance **plateaued beyond ~400,000–800,000 pretraining molecules**, and that a "baseline Random Forest using physicochemical descriptors remained the strongest overall model." — [Deep Learning Foundation Models from Classical Molecular Descriptors (arXiv 2506.15792)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.15792) *(see Gaps)*
- On the **TDC ADMET benchmark group**, roughly **75% of SOTA results** are held by "old-school" gradient-boosted trees over molecular fingerprints. — [ADMET Benchmark Group overview](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/admet-benchmark-group)
- Descriptor/fingerprint-based models outperformed graph-based models on 11 public datasets in accuracy *and* compute. — [GNNs vs Molecular Fingerprints 2025 benchmark](https://www.theochemtek.com/posts/graph-neural-networks-vs-molecular-fingerprints-a-2025-benchmark-for-molecular-property-prediction); reproducible companion: [admet-gnn-benchmark repo](https://github.com/drjoykarmakar/admet-gnn-benchmark)
- **Pat Walters' own 2026 head-to-head** ("Let the Agents Do the Benchmarking," 29 Aug 2026): baselines = Morgan FP + LightGBM, ChemProp single-task, ChemProp multi-task; foundation models = MEGA-CL (graph contrastive, ~100M molecules), Monroe (58.5M params, 1,152 tasks), Mol-JEPA (~50M params, JEPA), plus CheMeleon. Datasets: OpenADMET ExpansionRx blind challenge (9 endpoints; 5,326 train / 2,282 test) and the Biogen set (~3,521 molecules, 30% held out). — [Practical Cheminformatics](https://patwalters.github.io/Let-the-Agents-Do-the-Benchmarking/)
- Results of that benchmark: **Monroe + TabPFN** dominated LogD and microsomal stability on ExpansionRx and **outperformed all competitors on every Biogen assay**; ChemProp + CheMeleon won protein/tissue binding and LogS; **MEGA-CL and Mol-JEPA were best on no endpoint**. Walters' conclusion: "tabular foundation models paired with pre-trained representations yield impressive performance." — [Practical Cheminformatics](https://patwalters.github.io/Let-the-Agents-Do-the-Benchmarking/)
- Walters' general hierarchy of what matters in an ML model: **data > representation > algorithm**, with algorithms giving "smaller incremental improvements." — [Practical Cheminformatics](https://patwalters.github.io/)
- Walters on benchmark pathology: flawed benchmarks "create selection pressures favoring memorization over real insight" — framed as a critique of benchmarking methodology rather than of the models. — [Practical Cheminformatics](https://patwalters.github.io/)
- Walters on the hype cycle: "AI in Drug Discovery — Please Stop Fishing in the Bathtub!", noting that "every week purveyors of social media hype herald a new 'revolution'." — [Practical Cheminformatics](https://patwalters.github.io/)
- Related Walters posts useful for the "approximate checkers" argument: "Why Don't Machine Learning Models Extrapolate?" and "Three Papers Demonstrating That Cofolding Still Has a Ways to Go." — [extrapolation](https://patwalters.github.io/Why-Dont-Machine-Learning-Models-Extrapolate/); [cofolding](https://patwalters.github.io/Three-Papers-Demonstrating-That-Cofolding-Still-Has-a-Ways-to-Go/)
- There is live methodological dispute in the field about ADMET modeling itself: Walters published "My Response to Peter Kenny's Critique of OpenADMET." — [Practical Cheminformatics](https://patwalters.github.io/Response-to-Peter-Kenny/)
- Walters moved to OpenADMET ("Time For a New Adventure"), i.e. the leading public critic of foundation-model hype bet on **generating more data** rather than on better architectures. — [Practical Cheminformatics](https://patwalters.github.io/Time-For-a-New-Adventure/); [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/wpwalters)

### Inferences
- The pretraining plateau at ~10^5–10^6 molecules is the quantitative core of the data-scarcity thesis: there is no chemistry analogue of the text-scaling curve for molecular property prediction, because the *labels* (not the structures) are scarce. Unlabeled structures are effectively unlimited (Enamine REAL, GDB) and pretraining on them stops paying off quickly.
- The Monroe+TabPFN result reframes the problem: the win came from a model pretrained across **1,152 tasks** plus a *few-shot tabular* learner — i.e. meta-learning over many small assays, which is the correct inductive bias for scarce-label regimes. That is a meaningfully different bet from "train a big molecular LM."
- Against the user's thesis-as-stated: foundation models do have a defensible niche as *representations/oracles* inside the loop. The evidence does not support them as the reasoning layer.

### Gaps
- Two load-bearing numbers in this section (the "25 pretrained embedding models" study and the "400k–800k plateau") came back via search synthesis and I could only tie them to one plausible primary arXiv ID (2506.15792). **The report writer should verify these against the primary paper before quoting the figures.**
- I did not verify specific MolFormer / MoLFormer / ChemBERTa / Uni-Mol head-to-head numbers from their primary papers in this pass; they appear in the above surveys only indirectly.
- Polaris-hub leaderboard-level commentary (e.g. "no method beat simple baselines in challenge X") was not retrieved with a citable result table.

## Q4. Existing formal languages for chemical transformations usable as a medchem DSL

### Takeaway
There is no single established medicinal-chemistry *operations* language, but there is a mature and composable stack of pieces: SMILES/SELFIES for molecules, SMARTS for substructure patterns, **SMIRKS for transformations**, MMP transformations for empirical SAR edits, and Group SELFIES for fragment-level vocabularies. The 2025–2026 agent literature converges on exactly this — LLM agents that propose **matched-template transformations** at reactive sites rather than free-form SMILES — which is strong external validation of the DSL approach.

### Cited Findings
- **SMARTS vs SMIRKS:** "SMARTS and SMIRKS are two specific representations used for structural pattern searching and chemical reaction description, respectively. SMIRKS was introduced as an extension of SMARTS to represent chemical reactions." Template-based reaction methods use template sets encoding permitted changes, either expert-written or extracted from data via atom mapping. — [Molecular representations in bio-cheminformatics (Springer, 2024)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12293-024-00414-6)
- **SELFIES** (introduced 2020): "a language for molecules" guaranteeing **100% robustness** (every string decodes to a valid graph); primary motivation is use as direct input to generative ML models so that outputs are syntactically and semantically valid. — [SELFIES and the future of molecular string representations, *Patterns* (ScienceDirect)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2666389922002069)
- **Group SELFIES** (NeurIPS 2022 / arXiv 2211.13322): adds **group tokens** for functional groups and whole substructures while preserving robustness; exploits "the inductive bias of molecular fragments." Empirically, shuffling Group SELFIES tokens preserves chemically meaningful structures (e.g. benzene rings) far more often than shuffling atomic SELFIES. — [arXiv 2211.13322](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.13322); [NeurIPS page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2022/62789)
- There is active criticism of string representations as the substrate: "SMILES & SELFIES have to go: Representation of Molecules via Algebraic Data Types" argues for a typed/algebraic representation instead. — [arXiv 2501.13633](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.13633v2)
- **MMP-based LLM agents (2026):** "Reinforcement Learning with LLM-Guided Action Spaces for Synthesizable Lead Optimization" — a tool-augmented LLM agent "invokes specialized chemical analysis tools to identify reactive sites and propose chemically grounded transformations from **matched templates**," while a policy model trained with GRPO selects among the **constrained actions** to maximize long-term oracle reward. — [arXiv 2604.07669](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07669)
- **M3OS (2026):** Monte-Carlo-graph-search-orchestrated multi-agent LLM system for "evidence-traced" molecular optimization; integrates medchem reasoning with computational evidence over iterative multi-objective decisions, and "leverages matched molecular pairs to form annotated optimization graphs." — [arXiv 2609.34491](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34491)
- **TRACE (2026):** "Molecular Lead Optimization via Agentic Tool Planning" — trajectory-aware decisions over molecular optimization tools for forward-looking refinement under structural constraints; reports higher optimization success, larger property improvements, and higher validity on ADMET optimization tasks. — [arXiv 2605.28862](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.28862v1)
- **MolLEO** (ICLR 2025, "Efficient Evolutionary Search Over Chemical Space with Large Language Models"): uses the LLM **as a genetic operator** — crossover/mutation on molecular strings driven by a text prompt describing the objective. Improves over default Graph-GA and all other baselines with any of three LLMs tested; MolLEO(GPT-4) is best on **9 of 12** tasks, improving both final solution quality and convergence speed (fewer oracle calls). — [ICLR 2025 paper](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/file/804b5e300c9ed4e3ea3b073f186f4adc-Paper-Conference.pdf); [project page](https://molleo.github.io/)
- **DrugAssist** (*Briefings in Bioinformatics* 26(1), 2025): interactive molecule optimization via human–machine dialogue, leveraging LLM interactivity/generalizability; reports leading results on single- and multi-property optimization plus transferability and iterative optimization. — [Briefings in Bioinformatics](https://academic.oup.com/bib/article/26/1/bbae693/7942355)
- **GeLLM3O** (ACL 2025): generalizing LLMs for multi-property molecular optimization. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1225.pdf)
- **ToolMol** (2026): evolutionary agentic framework for multi-objective drug discovery. — [arXiv 2605.12784](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12784v1)
- **Agents on a Tree** (2026): pathwise coordination for multi-objective molecular optimization. — [arXiv 2606.00008](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.00008)
- **Synthesizability as a language constraint:** "Directly Optimizing for Synthesizability in Generative Molecular Design using Retrosynthesis Models" and "It Takes Two to Tango: Directly Optimizing for Constrained Synthesizability in Generative Molecular Design" — both make synthesis routes part of the optimization object rather than a post-hoc filter. — [arXiv 2407.12186](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.12186); [arXiv 2410.11527](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.11527)
- **Co-design of molecule and synthesis path:** "Compositional Flows for 3D Molecule and Synthesis Pathway Co-design" — generates the molecule *and* its synthesis tree together, i.e. the action space is reactions, not atoms. — [arXiv 2504.08051](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.08051)

### Inferences
- The strongest version of the user's thesis is available here: the field has independently, repeatedly arrived at "constrain the LLM's action space to chemically grounded transformations" (matched templates, MMPs, reaction templates, group tokens, synthesis trees) — four or five separate 2026 papers do this. A medchem-operations DSL is the generalization of that pattern, and the novelty claim should be about *unifying* these into one composable, verifiable action language rather than about having the idea at all.
- SELFIES' design principle — a grammar where every string is valid — is the right design target for a DSL: validity-by-construction removes a whole class of agent failure. Group SELFIES shows the same trick at the *fragment* level, which is the altitude medicinal chemists actually work at.
- Synthesizability-constrained generation suggests a DSL whose primitives are *synthetic* operations (apply this reaction to this building block) rather than *graph edits*, since the former is automatically make-able and the latter is not.

### Gaps
- I found no paper proposing a named, general-purpose "medicinal chemistry operations DSL" for LLM agents (closest are the MMP/matched-template action spaces above). This looks like a genuine white space, but absence of evidence after ~15 searches is weak evidence of absence.
- Enamine REAL, SynNet, and ChemReasoner specifically were not retrieved with primary citations in this pass.

## Q5. Checkers/oracles in drug discovery and reward hacking against them

### Takeaway
The oracle layer is real but approximate, and reward hacking against it is a documented, named failure mode — the field's answer is multi-objective constraints, uncertainty/applicability-domain awareness, and closing the loop with real experiments ("lab-in-the-loop"). Boltz-2 is the notable 2025 step change: FEP-approaching affinity accuracy at ~1000x lower cost, which makes a *usable* in-loop checker out of what used to be a batch-overnight calculation.

### Cited Findings
- **Boltz-2** (June 2025, biorxiv 2025.06.14.659707): "the first AI model to approach the performance of FEP methods in estimating small molecule–protein binding affinity," while being **at least 1000x more computationally efficient than FEP**. — [bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.14.659707v1); [PDF](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.14.659707.full.pdf); [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12262699/)
- Boltz-2 numbers: average **Pearson r = 0.66** on the 4-target FEP subset, outperforming all available inexpensive physical methods and ML baselines; approaches OpenFE (open-source relative FEP) on the full OpenFE benchmark; retrospectively outperforms all submitted entries on the **CASP16 affinity track** out of the box. — [Boltz-2 paper](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.14.659707.full.pdf); [code](https://github.com/jwohlwend/boltz)
- Boltz-2 coupled to a generative small-molecule model produced "diverse, synthesizable, high-affinity binders" on **TYK2**, with hits validated by absolute FEP simulations — i.e. a cheap oracle screened by an expensive oracle. — [Boltz-2 paper](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.06.14.659707.full.pdf); explainer [Rowan Boltz-2 FAQ](https://www.rowansci.com/blog/boltz2-faq)
- **Docking is exploitable:** "docking oracles can be highly exploitable, such that lipophilic molecules (lots of carbon atoms and high logP) with solubility issues receive good docking scores"; optimizing docking alone yields high-MW, low-QED molecules that score well but "are not meaningful molecules." — [Saturn: Sample-efficient Generative Molecular Design using Memory Manipulation (arXiv 2405.17066)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.17066)
- **Mechanism of reward hacking:** "molecular design using data-driven generative models is often susceptible to optimization failure due to reward hacking, where prediction models fail to extrapolate and fail to accurately predict properties for designed molecules that considerably deviate from the training data." — [A data-driven generative strategy to avoid reward hacking in multi-objective molecular design (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11897179/)
- Mitigations in use: multi-objective reward shaping (add QED maximization, MW caps) to force realistic output; applicability-domain/extrapolation awareness. — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11897179/); [Saturn](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.17066); on extrapolation generally [Walters](https://patwalters.github.io/Why-Dont-Machine-Learning-Models-Extrapolate/)
- Docking-score integration into generative AI with explicit interaction optimization (an earlier line of the same idea). — [PMC9629386](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9629386/)
- **Lab-in-the-loop** as the structural answer: FutureHouse's Robin is framed exactly this way ("Put the Lab in the Loop"), with the agent proposing and humans/instruments executing. — [DeepLearning.ai The Batch](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/put-the-lab-in-the-loop)
- Structure-prediction oracles are still not trustworthy enough to close the loop alone — three papers showing cofolding "still has a ways to go." — [Walters](https://patwalters.github.io/Three-Papers-Demonstrating-That-Cofolding-Still-Has-a-Ways-to-Go/)

### Inferences
- Reward hacking is the strongest argument *for* a constrained DSL over free generation: if the agent's only legal moves are chemist-recognizable transformations on a real starting point (MMP edits, applicable reactions), it cannot wander into the lipophilic-garbage region where the oracles are wrong. The DSL functions as a prior, not just an interface.
- The oracle stack is naturally tiered by cost (ML ADMET → docking → Boltz-2 → FEP → assay). A DSL plus agent makes that tiering explicit and schedulable; Boltz-2's 1000x cost reduction is what makes the middle tier affordable per-proposal.

## Q6. Data scarcity in small-molecule bioactivity, and efforts to fix it

### Takeaway
Public small-molecule bioactivity data is small in ML terms — ChEMBL 36 has ~24.5M bioactivity measurements across ~2.8M compounds and ~17,800 targets, and real ADMET benchmark sets are in the **thousands** of molecules, not millions. Several funded efforts (OpenADMET, Polaris, ASAP) are attacking this by generating internally consistent data at scale rather than by scaling models.

### Cited Findings
- **ChEMBL 36** (released 2025): ~**24.5 million bioactivities**, ~**2.8 million distinct compounds**, **17,803 drug targets**. — [EBI release note](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/about/news/updates-from-data-resources/chembl-36/); [Macs in Chemistry summary](https://macinchem.org/2025/09/26/chembl-36-is-out/)
  - *Note: one source says ChEMBL 36 was released in July 2025, another's post is dated 26 Sept 2025. Minor conflict on date; the counts agree.*
- ChEMBL's own positioning: "Evolving the Gold Standard in Bioactivity Data" (Zdrazil, 2nd ChEMBL UGM, 2026) — useful for current-state slide material. — [EBI slides](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/about/events/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/2-Barbara-Zdrazil_2nd-ChEMBL-UGM_ChEMBL_-Evolving-the-Gold-Standard-in-Bioactivity-Data.pdf)
- **How small real ADMET training sets are:** OpenADMET ExpansionRx blind challenge = **5,326 training molecules / 2,282 test** across 9 endpoints; the Biogen ADMET set = **~3,521 molecules** across 6 endpoints. — [Practical Cheminformatics](https://patwalters.github.io/Let-the-Agents-Do-the-Benchmarking/)
- **OpenADMET** is explicitly a *data engine* play: "developing a scaled and consistent data engine to support predictive ADMET models," sharing raw data, open-source models, and runnable code rather than static papers. — [Building the OpenADMET Data Engine](https://openadmet.ghost.io/building-the-openadmet-data-engine/); [OpenADMET](https://openadmet.ghost.io/)
- **Funding and scope:** Octant announced **$34.8M** in new funding for the OpenADMET initiative, supporting high-throughput data on membrane transporters, metabolic enzymes, hERG, and aminergic GPCRs, generating prospective ground-truth and structural data at scale. — [Digital Biology substack](https://digitalbiology.substack.com/p/roches-ai-labs-shared-antibody-data) *(secondary source; verify against Octant/OpenADMET primary announcement)*
- Specific OpenADMET releases: one of the largest publicly available internally consistent **CYP** datasets, and the largest high-quality **PXR induction** dataset ever released, with **>11,000 compounds**. — [OpenADMET](https://openadmet.ghost.io/)
- **Polaris / ASAP blind challenges:** the ASAP-Polaris-OpenADMET challenge used potency, ADMET, and crystallography data from ASAP Discovery's SARS-CoV-2 / MERS-CoV main protease program, run in three parts (ADMET, Potency, Ligand Poses) in early 2025; unblinded data is public. — [Polaris dataset](https://polarishub.io/datasets/asap-discovery/antiviral-admet-2025-unblinded); [polaris-recipes discussion](https://github.com/polaris-hub/polaris-recipes/discussions/32); [HF dataset](https://huggingface.co/datasets/openadmet/ASAP_Polaris_OpenADMET_challenge)
- The ExpansionRx-OpenADMET blind challenge was framed as "Advancing Predictive ADMET Modeling Through Community-Driven Science." — [HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/hugging-science/the-expansionrx-openadmet-blind-challenge); [OpenADMET announcement](https://openadmet.ghost.io/expansionrx-openadmet-blind-challenge/)
- OpenADMET's framing of the goal: turning ADMET "from an unpredictable gamble into a rational design problem." — [Digital Biology substack](https://digitalbiology.substack.com/p/roches-ai-labs-shared-antibody-data)

### Inferences
- The numbers support the data-scarcity thesis strongly: 24.5M heterogeneous, multi-assay, multi-lab bioactivity points is roughly four orders of magnitude short of what text pretraining consumes, and it is not i.i.d. — it's a sparse, biased matrix of compound x assay. The *effective* per-endpoint sample size is the 3,000–5,000 seen in ExpansionRx/Biogen.
- This is why the ether0 result matters more than the molecular-foundation-model results: at n≈5,000 per endpoint, the leverage is in the prior (reasoning + constrained action space + external checkers), not in the data.
- Conversely, the fact that the field's most credible skeptic (Walters) joined a *data-generation* consortium rather than a modeling group suggests the honest read is: a DSL-plus-agent buys you leverage now, but the binding constraint is still measurement.
